The emergencies we are called to in Windermere

Large lots and older landscaping shape the calls we get here. Tree roots find laterals. Irrigation lines run long and leak quietly. Water heaters sit in garages and closets far from a floor drain. The result is a mix of backed up lines, hidden leaks and failed tanks, and the common thread is that damage is slow to show until it is large.

Calls we take at any hour

Before We Arrive

Stop the water

Close the main valve at the house or the meter. If you are on a private well, switch off the well pump at the breaker.

Make it safe

Turn off power to wet rooms. Stay out of standing water near outlets.

Clear the way

Move cars from a driveway apron and open side gates. A technician may need to reach a cleanout.

Record what you see

Photograph stains, the meter and the damage for your insurer.

Wells, lakes and the water at your tap

Windermere sits between Lake Down and Lake Butler. The town incorporated on February 2, 1925, and counted 3,030 residents in 2020. The Orange Observer reported in April 2024 that most homes in town use well water and that the town sought a master water utility study at a cost of about 130,000 dollars.

That matters when something fails. A well household has a pressure tank and a pump, and a pressure problem may start there and not in the pipe. A well also delivers water that can carry more minerals, which shortens heater life and clogs fixtures. If you are on a well, say so when you call. Isleworth is unincorporated and Butler Bay was annexed in 2007, so ask us if you are unsure which water system serves you.

Windermere houses and what is inside the walls

The town covers older lakefront homes and newer estates. Older homes can have galvanized lines or cast iron drains that are near the end of their life. Newer homes have PEX or copper and long runs to large bathrooms, so a leak can travel before it shows. Large homes often have more than one water heater, and each is a possible failure.
