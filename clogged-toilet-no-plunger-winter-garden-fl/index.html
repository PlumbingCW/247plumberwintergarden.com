Clogged Toilet and No Plunger: A Winter Garden, FL Homeowner Guide

You can clear most clogged toilets without a plunger. Stop the water first, then try hot tap water with dish soap, a wire coat hanger wrapped in a rag or a wet dry vacuum. Boiling water can crack the bowl, so avoid it. If more than one drain is slow or the water keeps rising, the problem is deeper than the toilet and you should call a plumber.

First, stop the water from rising

Before you try anything, stop the tank from refilling. Lift the lid and push the flapper down, or turn the small valve behind the toilet clockwise. If the bowl is near the rim, stop flushing and wait. Flushing again only sends more water over the edge.

Put towels on the floor and open a window if the bathroom is small. Then pick one of the methods below. Work from the gentlest to the strongest.

Five ways to clear a toilet without a plunger

  1. Hot water and dish soapSquirt a generous amount of dish soap into the bowl. Pour a bucket of hot tap water from waist height. Let it sit twenty minutes. Soap lubricates the blockage and the weight of the water pushes it through. Do not use boiling water because porcelain can crack.
  2. Baking soda and vinegarA cup of baking soda followed by two cups of vinegar fizzes and may loosen a soft clog. It works best on paper and waste. It will not move a toy or a wad of wipes.
  3. A wire coat hangerUntwist a hanger, wrap the end in a rag and tape it so it will not scratch the bowl. Feed it into the drain opening and push and twist. This breaks up a soft blockage and can hook small objects.
  4. A wet dry vacuumIf you own a shop vacuum with a liquid setting, remove most of the water in the bowl, seal the hose against the drain and run it. It can pull a clog toward you. Use it only with a wet dry model.
  5. A bottle plunger trickA flexible plastic bottle with a sports cap can generate pulses of pressure when you push the water down. It is a last resort and results vary.

What to avoid

Mistakes that make it worse

  • Pouring in drain chemicals, which can sit in the bowl and burn skin.
  • Flushing again to see if it worked.
  • Using a metal tool that scratches the porcelain.
  • Pushing a stuck object further down the trap.
  • Ignoring a second slow drain in the house.

Toilets with a drain problem upstream behave differently from a toilet clogged in the trap. If the shower gurgles when you flush, or the tub water rises, the blockage is in the branch or main line and nothing you do in the bowl will fix it.

How to tell a toilet clog from a drain line clog

What you seeLikely locationWhat to do
Only this toilet is blockedTrap or closet bendTry the methods above.
Toilet and tub are slowBranch lineCall for drain cleaning.
Every drain is slowMain lineStop water use and call.

When to call a plumber

Call when two or more drains are slow, when sewage appears in a tub or a floor drain, when an object is stuck, or when you have tried the methods above and the water has not moved.
