Tankless Water Heater Gas Line: A Winter Garden, FL Homeowner Guide

A gas tankless water heater burns a lot of fuel in a short burst. Many units rate near 150,000 to 199,000 BTU per hour, while a typical tank heater is far lower. That means the gas line feeding it must be sized to deliver the volume, and many homes need a larger pipe. A plumber measures the run, counts every gas appliance and sizes the line before you buy a unit.

Why the gas line matters

A tank heater fires a modest burner to keep a large volume of water warm. A tankless heater fires a large burner to heat water as it flows. The burner needs a lot of gas at the moment you open a tap, and if the line can only deliver part of it, the heater fires weakly or shuts off. You get cold water in the middle of a shower.

That is why tankless installation starts with the gas line. The old line that fed a tank may be too small for a tankless unit, especially on a long run from the meter.

A plumber uses a sizing chart from the gas code or the heater manual to select the pipe diameter. Do not guess. An undersized line is the most common cause of a tankless heater that works part of the time.

Venting and combustion air

Gas tankless heaters produce hot exhaust that must exit the house safely. Models vary. Some use stainless steel vent pipe, some use plastic and some vent horizontally through a wall. A condensing model also needs a drain for acidic condensate. The vent route is part of the plan and has clearance rules from windows, doors and other openings.

Combustion air matters too. A heater in a closed closet may need a fresh air opening. We check these before we order a unit.

Gas, electric or hybrid: a quick comparison

Questions to answer before you commit

  • Is there gas service at the home, or only propane or electricity.
  • How far is the heater from the meter.
  • Can the existing line be enlarged or does a new run make sense.
  • Where can the vent exit.
  • Would an electric or hybrid tank be simpler for this house.

If gas is not an option, electric tankless models exist but need a large electrical service. See our water heater installation page for conventional choices.

What matters locally in Winter Garden

Source type: City building division and state licensing

A gas line change and a vent installation both need a permit and an inspection. The City of Winter Garden Building Division at 300 W Plant St handles permits, and unpermitted work can draw code enforcement. Check a contractor on the Florida DBPR Verify a Licensee page before hiring.

Many Winter Garden homes are slab on grade, so a new gas line often runs through an attic or along an exterior wall and not under the floor. That affects cost and routing. Ask your gas provider whether your home is on natural gas or propane, since the sizing tables differ.

Reading the sizing table

Gas codes publish tables that give the capacity of each pipe size at a given length. You add up the input of every appliance on the line, such as a furnace, a range, a dryer and the new heater, and match the total to the table at the length of the longest run.

A three quarter inch pipe may serve a modest run, while a one inch pipe may be needed for a long one or a large load. That is a quick check, and the installer verifies it with the actual layout.

Sediment traps and shutoff valves are part of a proper gas connection. The unit also needs a union so it can be removed for service.

What an installer should tell you

A good installer measures the run, counts appliances, calculates the load and tells you whether the line is adequate. If it is not, they explain the options. Ask for the calculation. Ask who handles the permit. Ask whether a pressure test is part of the job.

Our crews also handle plumbing installation when the project is bigger than a heater swap. We serve Horizon West and Windermere as well as the city.

Maintenance after the install

A tankless heater needs an annual flush in many homes, and the vent and filter should be checked. See our guide to tankless service for details. A gas line should be leak tested at install, and you should know where the gas shutoff is.
