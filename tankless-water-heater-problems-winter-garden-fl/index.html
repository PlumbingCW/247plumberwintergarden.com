Tankless Water Heater Issues: A Winter Garden, FL Homeowner Guide

Most tankless problems have a short list of causes. Scale, low flow, a dirty filter, a venting issue or a weak gas or power supply cover most of them. Note the error code and try cleaning the inlet filter. If the unit shows a code for ignition or flame failure, or if you smell gas, call a technician. A flush with descaler solves many cases.

The cold water sandwich

You take a shower, the water turns warm, then cold, then warm again. That pattern is called the cold water sandwich. It happens because hot water already in the pipe runs out, the unit takes a moment to ignite, and a small slug of cold water arrives. A unit with a buffer tank or a recirculation loop reduces it. Low flow fixtures can also cause it by dropping the flow below the unit's minimum.

A technician tests the minimum flow rate and checks the settings. Sometimes the fix is a setting. Sometimes it is a part.

Scale and the flush

Every tankless heater passes water through a narrow heat exchanger. Minerals coat the walls, shrink the passage and make the unit work harder. Error codes and noise follow. A flush uses a pump and a descaling liquid to dissolve the buildup. Run it yearly in many homes. Hard water needs more.

  2. Connect the hoses to the service portsA bucket and pump circulate the solution.
  3. Circulate for the time the maker recommendsTypically about an hour.

Gas, venting and power

A gas unit needs enough gas, a clear vent and combustion air. A blocked vent or an undersized line makes it shut off. An electric unit needs the right breaker and wire size. A loose connection or a tripped breaker leaves you without heat. If a unit has been working and suddenly does not, check the breaker and the gas shutoff before anything else, then call. For new work and gas line sizing, see water heater installation.

What matters locally in Winter Garden

Source type: City water quality report

The City of Winter Garden 2024 water quality report says the water is groundwater from the Floridan Aquifer, drawn through six deep wells and disinfected with sodium hypochlorite. It does not publish a hardness figure.

So test your own tap before you decide how often to flush. If you are on a private well, as many homes in Windermere are, mineral levels can be higher and flushing matters more. Our water treatment page covers options.

Buying a unit that fits your water and your house

Before you buy a tankless heater, ask about the minimum flow rate and the temperature rise. A unit that cannot heat the water as fast as it flows will hand you lukewarm water. Compare the flow rating at the temperature rise you need, and be skeptical of a rating given at a small rise. Look at the vent type and the condensate drain too.

Warranty terms often depend on a flush schedule. Keep a record of each service, including the date and the notes.

Venting problems you can spot

Look at the vent termination outside. Spider webs, leaves, wasp nests and snow in cold climates are not an issue here, but insects and debris are. A blocked vent causes a unit to lock out for safety. Look inside the unit area too. Soot, rust streaks or a smell of exhaust mean a technician should inspect it. Never block combustion air openings with storage.

A short maintenance calendar

Keep the unit healthy

  • Clean the inlet filter every few months.
  • Flush yearly, or more in hard water.
  • Check the vent and the condensate drain.
  • Keep the area around the unit clear.
  • Note the error history for a technician.

When to call

Call if the unit shows a flame or ignition error, if it leaks, if there is a smell of gas, or if a flush does not solve the problem. A tankless heater is a repair for a trained technician. We work on most brands and can tell you on the phone whether a visit is needed.
