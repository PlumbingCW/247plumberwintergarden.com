Common Causes of Clogged Drains: A Winter Garden, FL Homeowner Guide

Most clogged drains come from four things. Grease and food clog kitchen lines. Hair and soap clog tubs and showers. Wipes and excess paper clog toilets. Roots or low spots clog the main line. Most clogs build slowly. A drain that slows for weeks before it stops tells you to act now. A drain that clogs again after cleaning points to a defect in the pipe.

Kitchen: grease, food and soap

Fat leaves the pan hot and liquid, then cools and clings to the pipe. Each wash adds a layer until the opening is a fraction of its size. Coffee grounds, rice and pasta swell and mat with the grease. The result is a plug that a cable can open, but the layer on the pipe wall stays unless it is jetted.

Scrape plates into the trash, wipe pans with a paper towel and keep grease in a can. Hot water does not carry grease through a long line. It cools and settles farther down.

Bathrooms: hair, soap and objects

Hair and soap form a mat in a tub or sink trap. Toothpaste, floss and cotton swabs add to it. A drain cover that catches hair is the cheapest prevention. Pull the stopper and clean it monthly.

Toilets clog from too much paper, wipes labeled flushable and objects dropped by children. Nothing but toilet paper and waste should go down.

Prevention that works

Simple habits

  • Keep grease out of the kitchen sink.
  • Use drain screens in tubs and showers.
  • Flush only toilet paper.
  • Run hot water after washing a greasy pan.
  • Schedule a jetting for a restaurant style kitchen or a repeat problem.
  • Do not plant trees directly over the sewer line.

What matters locally in Winter Garden

Winter Garden went from 14,351 residents in 2000 to 46,964 in 2020. Newer slab homes have long horizontal drains, so a slight sag matters more than in a house with a short vertical stack. Mature oaks in older parts of town send roots to older clay and iron laterals.

If a defect needs digging, ask the City of Winter Garden Building Division at 300 W Plant St whether a permit is required. Unpermitted work can draw code enforcement and higher fees.

Laundry, floor drains and outdoor lines

Washing machines push lint and detergent residue into a standpipe that is only two inches wide. A lint trap on the hose catches some of it. If the standpipe overflows, the line past it is slow. Floor drains in garages collect sand, leaves and sediment, and a clogged garage drain turns a storm into a flood.

Outdoor drains and downspout lines fill with mulch, leaves and soil. Florida rain is heavy, and a buried downspout that backs up soaks the foundation. Clear the grates before the rainy season and flush the lines with a hose.

Warning signs worth acting on

Call earlier, not later

  • Gurgling sounds from a drain when another fixture runs.
  • Water rising in a tub when a toilet flushes.
  • Slow drains in more than one room.
  • A sewer smell near a floor drain.
  • A drain that clears with a plunger and clogs again in days.
  • Wet soil near the cleanout.

When one clog is a symptom

If every drain in the house is slow, the main line is involved. If a clog returns within weeks of cleaning, something in the pipe is catching debris. If you hear gurgling when a toilet flushes, the vent or the line is restricted. Call and describe the pattern.
