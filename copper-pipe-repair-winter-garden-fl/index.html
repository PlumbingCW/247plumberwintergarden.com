Copper Pipe Plumbing Repair: A Winter Garden, FL Homeowner Guide

You can repair a leaking copper pipe with a push fit coupling, a press fitting or a soldered coupling. First shut off the water and drain the line, then cut out the damaged section, clean the ends and install the fitting. A single pinhole may be a one time repair. Several pinholes in a short time are a sign that the pipe is wearing out and a repipe may be the better plan.

By Winter Garden 24/7 Plumbing. Published November 13, 2026.

Why copper leaks

Copper is durable, and it still wears out. Water chemistry, flow speed, flux left in joints and electrical grounding can cause pitting from the inside. A pinhole leak is the sign. Tiny sprays make green marks on pipes and stain drywall. Bad joints leak at the fittings. A freeze can split a pipe lengthwise, though freezes are rare here.

Signs of a failing copper pipe

  • Green or blue crust on pipes.
  • Damp drywall or ceiling stains.
  • Water pressure that drops over time.
  • Blue green stains on fixtures.
  • Hissing sounds in walls.

Repair methods compared

MethodHow it worksProsCons
Push fit couplingSlides onto clean pipe and gripsNo tools or heatCosts more per fitting, needs clean round pipe.
Press fittingA tool crimps a fittingFast, no flameNeeds a press tool.
Soldered couplingHeat joins a fitting to the pipeTraditional, low costNeeds skill, a dry pipe and caution with fire.
Repipe a sectionReplace a longer runRemoves the weak pipeMore work.

Soldering near a wall can start a fire, and wet pipe will not solder. A plumber handles those conditions daily. Our burst pipe repair team can fix the break and assess the rest.

A simple repair, step by step

  1. Shut off the water and drain the lineOpen a low faucet to empty the pipe.
  2. Mark and cut out the damaged sectionUse a tubing cutter for a clean square cut.
  3. Clean and deburr the endsRemove oxidation and sharp edges.
  4. Install the fittingPush or press it into place, or solder it.
  5. Restore the water and checkOpen slowly, then wipe the joint and watch for drips.

When one repair is not enough

One pinhole after decades of service is a repair. Three in a year is a pattern. Each repair opens a wall, and the repairs add up. A repipe with PEX or new copper often costs less than a string of repairs plus the damage. See our repiping page for the process.

Test your water if copper keeps failing. Aggressive water can pit pipes from the inside. Some homes also have grounding currents that accelerate corrosion, and an electrician can check.

What matters locally in Winter Garden

Source type: City water quality report and building division

The City of Winter Garden reports that its water is groundwater from the Floridan Aquifer and publishes no hardness figure in its 2024 water quality report. Chemistry varies by tap, so a test of your own water is a sensible first step if copper keeps pitting.

A repair of a short section does not usually need a permit. A repipe does. The Winter Garden Building Division at 300 W Plant St handles permits, and unpermitted work can draw code enforcement and higher fees.

Protecting copper going forward

Keep water pressure moderate with a working regulator, since high pressure and fast flow wear the inside of copper. Replace old shutoff valves that weep at the stem. Look at exposed pipe once a year and note any new green crust. A small stain caught early costs less than a ceiling.

If you add a water treatment system, make sure it does not make the water more aggressive toward copper. We can advise on that.

Choosing a plumber for a copper repair

Ask whether the plumber will test the rest of the line. Ask what fitting they use and why. Ask whether the repair is permitted when it needs to be. A good plumber explains why a pinhole opened and whether it is likely to happen elsewhere. A quick patch with no explanation may fail again next month.

Keep the cut out pipe. It tells us a lot about the cause.

Copper in older and newer Winter Garden homes

Historic homes near Plant Street may have copper added in a remodel, with galvanized pipe still in other parts of the house. Mid century homes elsewhere in the region used copper widely. Newer homes mostly use PEX or CPVC, so copper is less common in the subdivisions built after 2000. Tell us the age of your house when you call, and we will bring the right fittings.
