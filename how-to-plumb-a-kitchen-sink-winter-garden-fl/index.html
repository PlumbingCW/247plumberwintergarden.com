How to Plumb a Kitchen Sink: What Winter Garden, FL Homes Need to Know

A kitchen sink needs a handful of parts. Two supply lines with shutoff valves feed the faucet. A basket strainer, a tailpiece and a P trap carry the water to a vented waste line. The hot line goes on the left and the cold on the right. Most Winter Garden homes sit on a slab, so the drain usually comes out of the wall behind the cabinet. A permit may be needed if you move the plumbing.

The parts of a kitchen sink plumbing system

Think of the sink as two systems. The supply side brings hot and cold water to the faucet. The drain side takes it away. Each needs its own parts, and each has rules that keep water flowing and sewer gas out of the house.

Step by step for a standard single bowl sink

  1. Shut off the waterClose the main or the angle stops. Open the old faucet to drain the lines.
  2. Set the faucet and strainerInstall the faucet in the sink, then seat the basket strainer with putty or a gasket.
  3. Connect the supply linesAttach braided lines from the faucet to the angle stops. Hand tighten and add a quarter turn with a wrench.
  4. Build the drainConnect the tailpiece, the trap and the arm that runs to the wall. Keep the slope gentle and the trap level.
  5. Test for leaksOpen the stops, run water and watch every joint. Wrap a dry paper towel around each fitting to find weeping.

A double bowl sink adds a tee fitting that joins the two drains. A garbage disposal changes the layout again, and the dishwasher drain ties into the disposal or the tailpiece. If you plan either, read our guide to disposal service or ask us to handle the install.

Where the DIY ends

Replacing a faucet or a trap is within reach for many owners. Moving the sink, adding a new drain or changing the supply path is plumbing work that needs a permit in Winter Garden and an inspection. Cutting a slab to move a drain is a job for a plumber.

Florida law has its own view of unlicensed plumbing work for hire, and the permit rules matter if you ever sell the house. When in doubt, ask.

The air gap, the high loop and the vent

Dishwashers need an air gap or a high loop so dirty sink water cannot flow back into the machine. A high loop is a hose raised under the counter before it reaches the drain. The vent is just as important. Without it the trap can siphon dry and let sewer gas in.

Slab homes make venting a little more complicated, because the drain is below floor level and the vent may run up inside a wall. We trace the vent before we change anything.

What matters locally in Winter Garden

Source type: City building division and permit rules

The City of Winter Garden Building Division at 300 W Plant St handles permits. Work done without a permit can draw code enforcement and a fee of up to three times the permit fee. A Notice of Commencement is required for work over 5,000 dollars.

For a simple faucet swap you usually need no permit. For a moved drain or new supply line, ask the building division first, or have a licensed plumber pull it. The state DBPR Verify a Licensee page lets you check a contractor.

Choosing a sink, faucet and fittings that fit

Start with the sink. The number of holes in the deck decides which faucet fits. A three hole sink takes a faucet with a separate spray or a widespread set, and a single hole sink takes a single lever. Undermount sinks hang from the underside of the counter, so you connect the drain after the sink is set. A drop in sink drains the same way but is easier to swap.

Buy fittings that match. Plastic trap kits are standard in kitchens and cost little. Metal traps look better and last longer in an exposed cabinet. Whichever you choose, keep the trap arm short and sloped toward the wall. A long, flat arm collects grease and slows the sink.

Common mistakes

What we see when we are called to fix DIY work

  • Trap not level, causing slow drains.
  • Overtightened plastic nuts that crack.
  • No shutoff valves, so a failure means shutting the whole house.
  • Missing putty at the strainer, so the cabinet leaks.
  • Dishwasher hose lower than the drain with no loop.
