A tank that has failed leaves you without hot water, and in the worst case with a flooded closet. A failed tank is an emergency

Heaters fail in two ways. They stop heating, or they leak. A leak from the tank body is the end of the unit, and no repair will save it. Water can reach flooring, drywall and wiring quickly, so the first job is to stop the water and the power.

  1. Cold water valve offTurn the valve on the pipe above the tank clockwise.
  2. Power offSwitch the breaker off for electric, or set a gas control to pilot.
  3. Contain the waterUse towels and a wet vac if you have one.
  4. Call the 24/7 lineTell us the tank size and location so we can bring the right unit.

If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Choosing the right replacement

Most replacements match what was there. That keeps the connections and the venting familiar and the cost predictable. A larger family or a new bathroom may justify more capacity, and a garage with warm air may favor a heat pump model that uses less electricity.

Sizing and the details that trip people up

A tank that is too small runs out. One that is too large wastes energy. We size by the number of people in the house, the fixtures that run at once and the recovery rate of the new unit. A 40 or 50 gallon tank suits many Florida homes, but we measure and ask before we say so.

Other parts matter as much as the tank. A new temperature and pressure valve, a drain pan with a safe drain line, a thermal expansion tank where the system is closed, new flex connectors and a proper vent for gas models. We replace these as part of the job and explain each one.

Permits and local rules

A water heater replacement typically needs a permit. In Winter Garden the building division handles permits from 300 W Plant St, and unpermitted work can draw code enforcement and higher fees. We handle the permit steps we are responsible for, and the finished work should pass the city's inspection.

If you are hiring anyone else for this work, confirm the license on the Florida DBPR Verify a Licensee page.
