Double Sink Garbage Disposal Installation in Winter Garden, FL Homes

A disposal installs under one bowl of a double sink. The other bowl drains through a tailpiece and a waste arm that joins the disposal outlet before the trap. Power comes from a switched outlet or a hard wired circuit. The dishwasher drain hooks to a knockout port on the disposal. The job takes a couple of hours for a plumber and needs the right electrical setup.

How a double sink drains with a disposal

A double bowl sink has two drain openings. One gets a disposal mounted under the flange. The other gets a basket strainer and a tailpiece. Both flow into a single trap through a waste arm. The disposal has an outlet port that connects to that arm, and a second port that the dishwasher drain attaches to.

The layout must keep the trap lower than the disposal outlet and slope the arm toward the wall. A level or uphill arm collects waste and floods the other bowl.

Parts and tools

What you need

  • A disposal sized to the household, with a mount that fits the sink flange.
  • A disposal flange kit or the existing one.
  • A discharge elbow and a waste arm with a tee.
  • A tailpiece and trap kit.
  • Plumber putty and the gaskets that come with the unit.
  • A three prong cord kit or access to a switched outlet.
  • Basic hand tools and a bucket.

Step by step

  1. Turn off power and waterSwitch off the breaker for the sink outlet and close the angle stops.
  2. Remove the old drain and flangeDisconnect the trap and unscrew the flange mount.
  3. Mount the new flangePress plumber putty under the flange, seat it and tighten the mount from below.
  4. Attach the disposalLift the unit onto the mount, rotate to lock and align the outlet with the drain.
  5. Connect the drain and the dishwasherAttach the discharge elbow, run the waste arm to the other bowl's tailpiece and connect the trap. Remove the knockout plug if a dishwasher connects.
  6. Wire or plug in the unitUse a cord kit or an existing outlet. Never leave live wires exposed.
  7. TestRun water and the disposal. Check every joint for drips.

The dishwasher knockout and high loop

A new disposal has a plug in the dishwasher port. If you connect a dishwasher, you must knock out the plug with a screwdriver and hammer. Many people forget. When they do, the dishwasher cannot drain and the water backs up. Retrieve the plug from inside the disposal afterward.

The dishwasher hose should loop high under the counter before it reaches the disposal, or use an air gap, so that sink water cannot flow back. This is a code item.

What matters locally in Winter Garden

Source type: City building division and state licensing

Adding a disposal where none existed usually means adding an electrical outlet, and that is electrical work that may need a permit. The City of Winter Garden Building Division at 300 W Plant St handles permits. Unpermitted work can draw code enforcement and higher fees.

If you hire out the work, confirm the plumber and the electrician on the Florida DBPR Verify a Licensee page. Newer Winter Garden homes often have a switched outlet under the sink already, which makes a swap simple.

Choosing the disposal

A third horsepower unit handles light use. A half to one horsepower unit suits a busy kitchen and runs quieter. Continuous feed units start with a wall switch, and batch feed units run with a stopper in place, which some people prefer for safety with children. Check the mounting type against your sink flange, and note the discharge height so it clears the trap.

If your existing disposal is failing, see our repair page first. Some jams and resets are a quick fix.

Power, switches and safety

A disposal draws a few amps and should have its own protected circuit when possible. A wall switch controls it, and the switch must be reachable but not by a child at the sink. Cord kits are common and approved when used with a proper outlet. A hard wired unit needs a junction box under the sink with a cover. Never push wires into the unit casing without a proper connector.

If your kitchen has no ground fault protected outlet near the sink, ask an electrician to add one. It is a small cost for a wet location.

Common installation errors

Mistakes we see on repair calls

  • Trap arm sloping the wrong way.
  • Dishwasher knockout not removed.
  • Putty missing under the flange.
  • Overtightened mounting ring that cracks.
  • Wiring left hanging without a cover.
