The historic core of Winter Garden

Historic Downtown means the blocks around Plant Street, where the city grew up after it was incorporated in 1908. The Garden Theatre, the Edgewater Hotel and the Winter Garden Heritage Museum anchor the commercial side. Houses fill the streets around them.

The Winter Garden Historic Residential District is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and is roughly bounded by Plant, Boyd, Tilden and Central Streets. The city also keeps a separate Historic Downtown Architectural Overlay of about 116 acres with 270 residential and commercial properties. Its historic buildings mostly date from 1915 to 1940, and the oldest go back to about 1890.

We describe this area by those anchor streets because the overlay and the National Register district are drawn differently. If you are unsure which one your address falls in, the city can tell you. For you, that means a remodel near Plant Street can face different review than the same job in a newer subdivision, so ask before you plan one.

What an older house asks of a plumber

Age alone does not tell us what is inside a wall. Some homes here have been repiped, some have been partly updated and some have not. We do not assume any one material, and we check before we promise a fix.

A camera look at a drain or a pressure test on a supply line shows what you actually have. That is the reason a short inspection comes before a repair plan on an older house. If a line is near the end of its life, we explain the choices in plain terms and let you decide.

Permits and the historic overlay

The City of Winter Garden Building Division at 300 W Plant St handles plumbing permits. The city adopted Ordinance 10-05 on January 11, 2010, which sets standards for major improvements to existing structures in the Historic Downtown overlay. Its Architectural Review and Historic Preservation Board, created in 2009, reviews proposals such as new construction, demolition and renovation.

Most interior plumbing repairs are routine. If your plan changes the outside of a building, such as new vents or a relocated meter box, ask the city before work starts. We tell you when a job needs a permit and we handle the steps for our own work.

For utility problems, the city lists its water, sewer and stormwater service on its Utilities page. For a suspected leak on the city side of the meter, its public services line is 407-656-2256 during business hours.
