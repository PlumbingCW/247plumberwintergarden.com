Sewer Camera Inspection in Winter Garden, FL

A sewer camera inspection shows what is happening inside your line without digging. We feed a camera through the cleanout, record the run and mark any defect. It is useful before you buy a home, after a repeat backup or when you want to know whether jetting or repair is the right step.

Call (407) 964-8940

What the camera shows

A sewer camera is a waterproof lens on a flexible rod with a light and a locating transmitter. It travels the line from the cleanout toward the street and records what it sees. We look for roots, cracks, offset joints, sags that hold water, scale and debris, and the depth and position of each.

What an inspection can find

  • Roots entering at joints.
  • Cracked or collapsed sections.
  • Offset or separated joints.
  • Low spots that hold water.
  • Grease or scale buildup.
  • Connections that are not on a plan.

When an inspection is worth the visit

Emergencies are only one reason to book an inspection. Buying a home is the most common reason, since a sewer lateral problem is expensive and often invisible. A line that backs up twice in a season is the second most common reason. A third is a remodel that changes the plumbing layout and needs a map of what exists.

A camera also confirms the result of a cleaning. If the pipe looks clear after jetting, you know the repair is not needed. If it does not, you know before the next backup.

Inspection versus a plain cleaning

A cleaning answers one question, which is whether the line can be opened. An inspection answers a bigger one, which is why the line closed in the first place. If you have been cleared more than once, ask for the camera. It turns a guess into a recording you can watch with the technician and keep.

We also use the camera to check work. After a repair or a jetting visit, a second pass confirms that the pipe is clear and that the repair holds. That is how you know the money went to the right fix and not to a second round of cleaning.

Buying a home in West Orange County

The area grew from 14,351 people in Winter Garden in 2000 to 46,964 in 2020, and many homes changed hands during that growth. A home inspector does not usually scope the sewer lateral. A camera inspection before closing gives you a recording to use in negotiating a repair or credit.

Historic homes near the downtown district may have older pipe material. Newer subdivisions may have builder grade plastic laid on fill that has settled. Both are worth a look.
