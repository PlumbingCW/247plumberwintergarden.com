Plumbing Installation in Winter Garden, FL

New bathrooms, kitchen remodels, laundry rooms and additions all need plumbing installed to code. We handle fixtures, supply and drain lines and rough in for remodels and additions in Winter Garden. A planned job gets a plan, a permit and an inspection.

What counts as a plumbing installation

Installation covers new work as opposed to repair. Adding a second bathroom, moving a kitchen sink, putting a utility sink in the garage, roughing in plumbing for a pool bath or running a line to an outdoor kitchen are all installations. Each touches supply, drain and vent, and each needs the right slope and the right size pipe.

Typical installation jobs

  • New bathroom or half bath.
  • Kitchen remodel with moved sink or dishwasher.
  • Laundry room hookups.
  • Outdoor kitchen or hose bibs.
  • Ice maker and filter lines.
  • New fixture sets in a remodel.
  • Plumbing for an addition or garage conversion.

Slab homes and what remodeling means here

Most Winter Garden homes sit on slabs. Moving a drain means cutting concrete, which costs more than in a house with a crawl space. Adding a bathroom in the middle of a slab home often means a plan to tie into existing lines at a good point. Sometimes we can place new fixtures against an existing wet wall and avoid cutting the slab.

Historic homes near the downtown district have their own rules and surprises. Walls may hide old pipe, and a remodel can reveal it. We check what is there before we promise a layout.

Winter Garden's building division at 300 W Plant St handles permits. Work without a permit can draw code enforcement and higher fees, and unpermitted work can complicate a sale or an insurance claim.

Fixtures, vents and drains: the parts people forget

Every drain needs a vent so wastewater can flow and so sewer gas stays out of the house. Vents run up through the roof or tie into another vent, and a remodel can change what is possible. A drain needs slope too. A quarter inch per foot is the usual rule for many drain pipes, and a long run on a slab leaves little room for error.

Supply lines need shutoff valves at each fixture, and we install those as standard. We also size the lines so a new shower does not starve the house of pressure when the dishwasher runs.

Kitchen, bath and laundry details

A kitchen sink with a disposal and a dishwasher needs an air gap or a high loop, a separate drain tie in and a place to plug in power. A bathroom needs a proper trap for each fixture and a rough in for the shower valve at the height of your chosen trim. A laundry room needs a recessed box for the washer and a drain that can handle the discharge.

We measure to your fixture specifications, so give us the model numbers before the walls close. That one step prevents most of the surprises in a remodel.

Working with your contractor

Remodels involve several trades. We coordinate with your general contractor, tile setter and electrician so the plumbing is where the walls need it. Tell us which fixtures you have chosen, since spacing and rough in dimensions depend on the model.

For existing pipe problems found during the remodel see repiping and water line repair.
