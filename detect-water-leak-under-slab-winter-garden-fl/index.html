Detecting a Water Leak Under the Slab for Winter Garden, FL Homes

A slab leak announces itself in a few ways. The meter runs with every fixture off. Floor spots feel warm. Water sounds at night. Flooring turns damp. Confirm with a meter test, then call a plumber who can pressure test the lines and use listening equipment to find the spot. Shut off the main if water is pooling.

Signs you can check in an evening

Walk the house and note

  • A warm patch of floor, especially in a hallway or kitchen.
  • Damp carpet or tile that sounds hollow.
  • A musty smell with no visible source.
  • Baseboards that swell or separate from the wall.
  • The sound of water when the house is quiet.
  • A water bill that rose without a change in use.

Do not pull up flooring to look. A plumber will find the spot with equipment and a small opening beats a large one.

Confirm with the meter and a pressure test

Close every fixture and check the meter for movement. Then close the main valve at the house and check again. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house or in the line after the shutoff. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the buried supply line before it.

A plumber separates hot from cold. A pressure gauge on a hose bib shows whether the system holds pressure when isolated. Hot lines and cold lines are tested separately because a hot line leak behaves differently.

A good locate marks the leak on the floor within a foot or so, so an opening is small. Our leak detection page covers the full process, and slab leak repair lays out the repair choices.

Why slabs leak in West Orange County

Central Florida homes sit on slabs over sandy soil. Pipes in or under the slab can chafe, corrode or fail at a joint. Soil movement after heavy rain stresses lines, and some builders ran pipe with tight turns that wear over time. A line that sat quietly for twenty years can fail suddenly after a shift.

The cause also matters for the fix. A chafed line may fail again. A corroded section may mean other sections are close behind.

Reading your water bill for clues

Your utility bill is a free leak detector if you compare months. Look at the usage line, not the dollar amount. A jump of a few thousand gallons that does not match your habits suggests a leak. Irrigation, a pool fill or guests can explain a rise, so rule them out. A leak shows up as steady extra use across a month.

Keep old bills and compare the same month across years. Many utilities offer an online usage graph that shows overnight use. A flat line near zero overnight is normal. A steady trickle overnight is a leak.

After the repair

Once the leak is fixed, watch the meter for a few days. It should sit still when you are away. Keep the written report from the plumber with photos and the leak location. It supports an insurance claim, a builder warranty claim or a future sale. If flooring or drywall was wet, a restoration company should dry it and check for mold.

Costs of waiting

A slab leak wastes water every hour and can erode soil beneath the foundation. Flooring, baseboards and drywall suffer. Mold can follow within days in warm, wet conditions. Acting early limits all of it. Insurance companies also expect you to act promptly to stop damage.
