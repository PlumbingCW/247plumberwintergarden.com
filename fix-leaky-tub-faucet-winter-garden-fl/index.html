How to Repair a Leaky Tub Faucet: A Winter Garden, FL Homeowner Guide

A dripping tub faucet usually has a worn cartridge, washer or seat. Shut off the water, remove the handle, pull the cartridge or stem and replace the worn part with an exact match. The job takes under an hour if the valve is accessible. If the shutoff does not work or the valve is behind tile, call a plumber.

Find out which kind of faucet you have

Tub faucets come in four main types. A compression faucet has two handles that you turn several times. A cartridge faucet has one handle and a cartridge. A ball faucet is common in kitchens and rare in tubs. A ceramic disk faucet has a cylinder with discs inside. Knowing the type tells you what to replace.

TypeLook and feelUsual fix
CompressionTwo handles, many turnsReplace the washer and seat.
CartridgeOne handle, smoothReplace the cartridge and O rings.
Ceramic diskOne handle, quarter turnReplace the disc cartridge.

What you need

Tools and parts

  • Screwdriver set and an adjustable wrench.
  • Allen keys for set screws.
  • A cartridge puller if you have a cartridge faucet.
  • Plumber grease for O rings.
  • The exact replacement part, taken from the old one.
  • Towels to cover the drain so screws do not fall in.

Step by step

  1. Shut off the waterUse the bathroom shutoff if there is one, or the main. Open the faucet to release pressure.
  2. Remove the handlePry off the cap, remove the screw and lift the handle.
  3. Remove the trim and stem or cartridgeUnscrew the escutcheon, then pull the cartridge or unscrew the stem.
  4. Inspect and replaceLook for worn washers, a cracked O ring or scale. Replace the part and clean the seat.
  5. Reassemble and testReverse the steps, open the water slowly and watch for drips.

If the faucet still drips after a new cartridge, the valve body may be damaged. That can mean opening the wall, which is a plumber's job. See our faucet repair page and our leak detection page if you find moisture behind the tub.

Fixing a compression tub faucet

Compression faucets are common in older homes and the repair is simple. After you remove the handle and unscrew the stem, look at the rubber washer at the bottom. If it is flattened or cracked, replace it with the same size. Then check the brass seat that the washer presses against. A rough seat will cut a new washer, and a seat tool or a replacement seat fixes it.

Coat the new washer and the stem threads with plumber grease. Hand tighten the stem, reassemble and open the water slowly. If the faucet drips again right away, the seat is probably still damaged.

Fixing a single handle cartridge faucet

Single handle faucets hide a cartridge behind a retaining clip or nut. Pull the clip with pliers and use a cartridge puller if the part sticks. Note which way the cartridge faces before you remove it, because the hot and cold sides matter. Install the new cartridge facing the same way, replace the clip and test the temperature.

If the faucet runs hot when set to cold, the cartridge is rotated or the stops are reversed. Pull it out and set it again. Replacement parts must match the brand and model, since cartridges are rarely interchangeable.

Why scale matters here

Mineral scale glues parts together and wears seals. The city of Winter Garden reports that its water is groundwater from the Floridan Aquifer, and it does not publish a hardness figure. Check your own tap. If you see white crust on the trim, scale may be shortening the life of your cartridge.

Soak a stuck stem or cartridge in vinegar before you force it. Avoid pliers on chrome trim because they leave marks.

When to call a plumber

Call if the shutoff will not close, if the valve is corroded, if water appears in a wall or ceiling below, or if the faucet leaks from the back of the wall. A leaking tub valve can rot a wall quietly, and it is cheaper to fix early. For older homes in Winter Garden's historic district, valves may be old and parts may need to be matched by hand.
