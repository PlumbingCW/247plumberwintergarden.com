The first ten minutes of a burst pipe

What you do before we arrive decides how much damage there is. The main shutoff is usually at the street meter box, on the side of the house or near the water heater. Learn where it is now, and test it once so it turns when you need it.

  1. Shut the main valveTurn it clockwise until water stops.
  2. Cut power to wet areasUse the breaker, and do not touch wet outlets.
  3. Open a low faucetIt relieves pressure and drains what is left in the line.
  4. Photograph everythingInsurers want to see the damage before you clean up.
  5. Call the 24/7 lineTell us where the water is coming from.

Why pipes burst in a warm climate

Winter Garden has a history of freezes. The citrus freezes of 1894 and 1895 and again in the 1980s are part of local lore, and a hard freeze can still catch exposed lines in a garage or on an outside wall. Freezing is not the main cause today. Most bursts here come from corrosion, high pressure, failed fittings and age.

Repair choices

A burst on a short, accessible section is a quick repair. A pipe that has burst once and is old will probably burst again, so we tell you if the section is a symptom of a wider problem. Sometimes repairing the break and watching the system is right. Sometimes a repipe saves you from a series of emergency visits.

When a burst floods a space, we stop the water and make the line safe first. Water extraction and drywall are for a restoration company, and we can describe the plumbing findings to them and your adjuster.

Pressure, regulators and the quiet causes

Florida utilities supply water at pressure that can exceed what older fixtures and supply lines are built for. A failed or missing pressure regulator can push a weak spot past its limit. We test your pressure on the call, and a regulator replacement is often a cheap way to protect pipes that have not yet failed.

Hammer noises when a valve closes and an expansion tank that has lost its charge are other warning signs we look for.
