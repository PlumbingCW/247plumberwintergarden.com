When a hidden leak becomes an emergency

A damp spot is a clue and not always a crisis. A ceiling stain that grows, a wall that feels warm or a floor that has started to lift are crises. So is a meter that spins when every fixture is off. Find the main shutoff and learn where it is before you need it.

Do this while you wait

  • Turn off the main water valve at the house or the street meter.
  • Switch off power to any wet room before touching anything.
  • Move furniture and rugs away from damp areas.
  • Take photos of stains and the meter reading.
  • Do not cut into drywall, because we locate the source first.

The meter test and the high water bill

The cheapest leak detector is the water meter. Turn off every fixture and appliance, then watch the meter. Most meters have a low flow indicator, a small triangle or dial that moves when any water passes. If it moves with everything off, water is leaving the system somewhere between the meter and your fixtures.

That tells you a leak exists. It does not tell you where. We take it from there with acoustic listening, thermal imaging and pressure testing of the lines, then isolate the section and mark the spot before anything is opened.

Where leaks hide in Winter Garden homes

Most local homes sit on concrete slabs, so supply lines often run in or under the slab, and drains run in it. Older homes in the Historic Residential District may have crawl spaces, where a leak can run for months before it reaches a floor. Ceilings and attic runs show leaks as stains and sagging.

Irrigation, pools and yards

Sprinkler leaks are common in Florida because systems run often and valves, heads and buried lines wear. A zone that stays wet after the cycle ends is a good lead. Pool and spa leaks are a different specialty, so we tell you if the loss is in the plumbing lines to the equipment or in the shell, and refer you accordingly when the shell is the cause.

A leak at the meter box or on the yard side of the shutoff may be the utility's responsibility rather than yours. Photograph it, note the meter reading and call the city before you pay for a repair.
