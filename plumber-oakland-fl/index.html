A town with a long history and mixed plumbing

Oakland was incorporated in 1887 and had a population of 3,516 in the most recent census. Its historic Town Hall dates from 1912, and the West Orange Trail passes through the Oakland Nature Preserve. Freezes in the 1890s and a fire shaped the early town. The homes that remain run from historic cottages to new subdivisions on the edge of town.

That variety matters to a plumber. A cottage with galvanized pipe, a mid century house with copper and a new home with PEX all fail differently. We ask the age of the house when you call so the right parts are on the truck.

Old pipe, new pipe and what a plumber looks for

When we open a wall in an older Oakland home, we look at three things. First, the supply material, because galvanized steel closes from the inside and bursts at weak points. Second, the drain material, because cast iron develops scale and cracks near joints. Third, the connections between old and new, where a remodel joined different metals and left a spot that corrodes.

A repair on an old house works best when it respects the pipe around it. We explain what a fix will do and what it will not do, so you can decide between a single repair and a plan to replace more.

The town water system

Oakland runs its own water system. If you see water at the meter or in the street, call the town. If the water is on your side, call us. A burst before the meter is the town's, and one after it is yours.

Because the system is small, pressure can differ from larger utilities. If low pressure shows up across the house, we test at a hose bib and look at your regulator before assuming a leak.
