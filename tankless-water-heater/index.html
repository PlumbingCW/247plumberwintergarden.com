Tankless Water Heater Service in Winter Garden, FL

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows, so it does not run out and it takes little space. It also needs more attention than a tank. We install tankless units, repair them and flush and descale them in Winter Garden.

What a tankless heater needs from your home

A tankless unit fires a large burner or a powerful element only when water flows. That means the gas line, the venting or the electrical service must supply a lot at once. A house with a small gas line or an undersized panel may need upgrades before a tankless unit can work properly.

We check the supply, the venting route and the water flow rate before we recommend a model. A unit that is too small leaves you with a cold shower when two fixtures run at once.

Flushing and descaling: the part owners skip

Hot water leaves minerals behind. In a tank they fall to the bottom. In a tankless unit they coat the heat exchanger and narrow the passages, which causes error codes, reduced flow and noisy operation. A flush with a descaling solution removes the buildup.

The city of Winter Garden reports that its water is groundwater from the Floridan Aquifer and publishes no hardness figure. Mineral content varies at each tap, so test yours. Hard water means flushing more often, and a softener may help, as described on our softener and filtration page.

Most tankless problems are not emergencies. If you have no hot water and the unit shows a code, we can treat it as a repair visit. A leak is different, so shut the water and power and call the line.

Installing a tankless unit

Installation is a bigger job than swapping a tank. It can require a new gas line, a vent through the wall or roof, a condensate drain for high efficiency models and a larger circuit for an electric unit. We explain these needs in plain terms and give the price before we begin.

For a conventional swap see water heater installation. If your tank has failed and you want hot water today, our replacement team can often help the same day.

Sizing a unit for the way you live

Tankless sizing starts with flow and temperature rise. We add up the fixtures that may run together, such as a shower, a kitchen tap and a dishwasher. Then we match that flow to a unit that can lift the incoming water temperature to what you want. Florida groundwater is warm for much of the year, which helps, but it is not an excuse to undersize.

A large household with two showers running at once may need a bigger unit or two units. A couple in a small home may be well served by a compact model. We would rather size it right than sell the largest box on the shelf.
